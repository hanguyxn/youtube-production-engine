# Editorial and learning

## Research and script

Define the viewer question, promise and scope before writing. Use original research and authoritative institutions for factual claims, with scholarly synthesis for context. Source hierarchy varies by profile: a historical reconstruction needs different evidence from a software demonstration. Read the relevant source, not just a snippet; disclose abstract-only access.

Save a ledger in `research.md`: claim, source URL/DOI and passage/figure, what it supports, date/scope, uncertainty, intended wording. Distinguish direct evidence, interpretation, modern analogy and illustrative reconstruction. Verify numbers, dates, superlatives, causal claims and quotations individually. Revise a premise that the evidence does not support. Do not invent sources, access or expert consensus.

Develop title/thumbnail promise early but keep it provisional. Consider two opening approaches and select the one that delivers useful evidence or a partial answer quickly. Aim to establish the concrete question in the first 5–10 seconds and advance the explanation within the first minute; these are editorial guides, not algorithm thresholds. Avoid mandatory vignettes, rhetorical-question quotas and repeated stakes.

Each section should add a discovery, mechanism, complication or resolution. Explain technical terms on first use. Resolve the actual question at the end. Use the profile's tone without forcing every episode into the same arc. Preserve a strong supplied script.

Choose duration from the content and the profile's flexible range. Without measured speech use 150 wpm provisionally; generated duration wins. Save spoken language only in `script.txt`; keep citations, pronunciation notes and visual instructions elsewhere. Before voice, check support for claims, title alignment, repetition and comprehension on one listen.

## Packaging

Offer distinct title/thumbnail hypotheses that the episode actually fulfills. Use the profile's concept count and angles only where relevant; a calm discovery need not become a threat scene. No fabricated statistics, danger or certainty. Keep titles readable (about 65 characters when practical). Thumbnail text should add to the title, remain readable on mobile and avoid the duration badge. Check actual generated thumbnails; use editable text if generated typography fails.

In `metadata.md` include candidate titles, concise description, relevant source links, and chapters from final SRT. Without final timing deliver a clearly labeled untimed outline. Follow current YouTube chapter rules when claiming chapters are ready for upload (first starts 00:00, at least three, each at least 10 seconds). Optional tags/hashtags must be relevant. Thumbnail prompts are self-contained, copyable, and include the profile's character/style details. Record title pairing, viewer question and evidence promised for each concept.

## Analytics

Record date window, video age, processing delays, impressions, CTR by traffic source, AVD, percentage viewed and early retention where available. Compare similar ages and lengths. Realtime counts are distinct from processed reports. Small samples support questions rather than causal conclusions. Do not infer shadowbanning from low views, or interpret AVD as every viewer's exit point.

Save an explicit experiment record: baseline/window/sample, hypothesis, one principal change, expected observation and comparable review point. Multiple simultaneous changes are exploratory. Channel learning may use user-supplied experiment records without browsing unrelated project directories.
